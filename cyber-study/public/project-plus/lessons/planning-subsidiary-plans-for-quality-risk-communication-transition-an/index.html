CompTIA Project+ PK0-005 · Domain 2: Project life cycle phases

Planning: subsidiary plans for quality, risk, communication, transition and release

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 7, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

A project plan is not just a calendar. It is a binder of smaller plans, each explaining how one part of the project will be handled. The quality plan says what 'good enough' means and how it will be checked. The risk plan explains how the team will spot and rate risks. The communication plan says who hears what, when and how. The transition plan explains how the finished system will be handed to the people who will support it every day. The release plan explains how users will get the new system, all at once or a group at a time, and how to undo it if things go wrong. It is like opening a new restaurant: you need a menu, but also rules for hygiene, staff training and a soft opening.

It is 7:40 on the Monday morning after go-live of Summit Transit's new expense system, and the service desk queue already has ninety tickets. The analysts have never seen the new screens. There are no runbooks. The vendor's developers rolled off the project on Friday. When a supervisor asks whether the old system can be switched back on, nobody knows if that was ever planned, or who would decide. You are standing beside the service desk manager as her phone keeps ringing, and she asks the question you were hoping to avoid: 'Who was supposed to get us ready for this?' Which plans should have answered her weeks ago?

The project management plan is not one document but a collection: the baselines for scope, schedule and cost plus subsidiary plans that describe how each area will be managed. On a small project each subsidiary plan may be a page; on a large one it may be substantial. Writing them forces the team to think through how the project will run before problems arise, and it gives new team members and auditors a single place to see how decisions are made. Like the baselines, the plan is approved and then updated through change control, so it stays a reliable reference rather than a forgotten draft.
