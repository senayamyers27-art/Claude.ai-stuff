CompTIA Project+ PK0-005 · Domain 1: Project management concepts

Resource management: resource types, allocation, leveling vs smoothing, shared resources and RACI charts

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 7, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Resources are everything a project needs: people, equipment, rooms, software licenses and money. Resource management means getting the right ones at the right time and not overloading anyone. When a person has more work than hours in a week, you can fix it two ways. Smoothing shuffles tasks only into spare gaps so the finish date stays the same. Leveling moves tasks as far as needed, even if the finish date slips. To avoid confusion about who does what, teams use a RACI chart: who does the work, who owns the result, who gives advice and who just needs to be told. Think of a family moving house: one person packs the kitchen, one parent signs off on the moving truck, Grandma is asked where the china goes, and the neighbors are just told the date.

Priya is the only engineer at Bayview Logistics certified on the new firewall platform, and your rollout to 40 branches depends on her. You open the resource histogram for week six and see a bar at 70 hours: two branch cutovers and a design review all landed on her at once. She is also on the operations on-call rota that week. Her manager has already told you that outages come first. Meanwhile, two people both believe they own sign-off on the cutovers, and a third assumed someone else was telling the branches. Can you fix Priya's week without moving the end date, and who is actually in charge of what?

Start with what counts as a resource. Resources are everything the project needs to do its work: people (human resources), equipment such as laptops and test servers, facilities such as a training room, materials, software licenses and money. Physical resources need ordering, storing and tracking; human resources need skills, availability and motivation. Resources can be dedicated to the project full time or shared with operations or other projects. Shared resources are a frequent source of conflict: a network engineer who also handles outages may be pulled away at the worst moment, so the project manager negotiates availability with functional managers and records it in a resource calendar that shows when each person or asset is available, including holidays, on-call weeks and maintenance windows.
