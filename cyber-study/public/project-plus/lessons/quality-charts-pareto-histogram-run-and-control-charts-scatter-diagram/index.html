CompTIA Project+ PK0-005 · Domain 3: Tools and documentation

Quality charts: Pareto, histogram, run and control charts, scatter diagrams and fishbone (Ishikawa) diagrams

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 7, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Quality charts turn a pile of numbers into a picture that answers one question. A histogram counts how often things fall into groups, like how many tickets took one hour or two hours. A Pareto chart is the same, but sorted from biggest to smallest, so you can see the few problems causing most of the pain. A run chart shows a number over time, and a control chart adds limits that show what normal looks like, so you can tell if something has changed. A scatter diagram checks whether two things move together. A fishbone diagram is a structured brainstorm of possible causes. Picture a doctor: a fever chart over several days, a list of symptoms by how common they are, and a list of possible causes to test.

It is the monthly service review at Pinecrest County Schools, three weeks after 2,000 new student laptops were deployed. The help desk lead slides a spreadsheet of 600 tickets across the table and says the rollout was a disaster. The vendor says the numbers are normal for a launch. The superintendent wants to know what to fix first and whether things are getting better or worse. Everyone has an opinion, and nobody has a picture. You have the data. Which chart turns that spreadsheet into an answer each person in the room will accept?

Quality tools help a team understand problems using data instead of opinions, and they are used in quality control activities while the project is executing as well as in planning how quality will be measured. On the exam you will usually be given a situation, or a description of a chart, and asked which tool fits. So for each chart, learn the question it answers: how values are distributed, which causes matter most, whether a process is stable over time, whether two variables are related, or what might be causing a problem.
