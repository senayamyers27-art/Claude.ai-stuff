Types of change: scope creep, gold plating, timeline, budget, resource, requirements and emergency changes

Explain it simpler

Projects change in different ways, and each kind has a name. Scope creep is when customers keep asking for little extras and they get added without anyone approving them. Gold plating is when the team adds extras nobody asked for because they think it will impress. Other changes hit the dates, the money, the people or the requirements. Emergency changes must happen fast to prevent harm, like fixing a broken lock right away, but they still get written up afterward. Picture a friend painting your room. If you keep saying 'while you are here, could you also do the hallway', that is scope creep. If your friend paints a mural you never wanted, that is gold plating. Either way the job takes longer than agreed.

You are three weeks from go-live on a warehouse scanning app for Cedar Lane Distribution. The schedule says you should be in final testing, but the build keeps changing. Floor supervisors have been asking the developer for 'just one more button', and he has been happy to help. A tester has built an animated dashboard nobody requested. This morning the security team announced a critical flaw in the scanner firmware and wants it patched before anything ships. Your sponsor asks a simple question: why are we behind, and what are we doing about it? Which of these is which kind of change, and how should each one be handled?

Start with the big picture. Not all changes look the same, and the Project+ exam expects you to recognize the common types and how each should be handled. The idea behind all of them is the same: any change to what the project delivers, when it delivers or what it costs should pass through change control so the trade-offs are visible. What differs is where the change comes from, how urgent it is and which constraint it hits first.
