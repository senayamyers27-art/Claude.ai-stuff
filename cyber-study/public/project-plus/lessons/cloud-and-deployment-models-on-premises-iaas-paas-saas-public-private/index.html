CompTIA Project+ PK0-005 · Domain 4: Basics of IT and governance

Cloud and deployment models: on-premises, IaaS, PaaS, SaaS, public, private and hybrid cloud, and their trade-offs

▶ Watch the overview video

Explain it simpler

Cloud computing means renting computing from a provider instead of owning all the equipment yourself. On-premises means you own and run everything in your own building. With IaaS, you rent the basic machines and handle everything that runs on them. With PaaS, the provider also runs the operating system, and you just bring your own program. With SaaS, you simply use a finished application, like web-based email. The more the provider does, the less you do, but you are always responsible for your own data and who can log in. Think of food: cooking at home is on-premises, renting a kitchen is IaaS, a meal kit is PaaS, and eating at a restaurant is SaaS. You still choose who sits at your table.

The email servers at Cedar Valley Community College are five years old and the vendor has announced the end of support. In the planning meeting, Ray from IT wants to buy new servers, Lena from finance asks why the college would spend a large sum up front when a monthly subscription exists, and the registrar wants to know who will be blamed if student emails leak. Someone says 'just move it to the cloud', as if that ends the conversation. You know it is really the start of one. Which cloud model fits, who is responsible for what, and how does the choice change the budget and the plan?

Many projects today involve the cloud, either moving existing systems there or adopting new cloud services. The model chosen changes the work to be done, the costs and how they are paid, the risks, and who is responsible for what. The exam tests the service models, the deployment models and the trade-offs a project manager must plan for. Think of two separate questions: what does the provider manage for us (the service model), and who shares the cloud and where does it run (the deployment model).
