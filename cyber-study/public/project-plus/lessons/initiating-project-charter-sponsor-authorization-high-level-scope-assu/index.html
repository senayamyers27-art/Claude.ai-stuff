Initiating: project charter, sponsor authorization, high-level scope, assumptions, constraints and success criteria

▶ Watch the overview video

Explain it simpler

Starting a project officially is a bit like getting a permission slip signed. The project charter is a short document that says 'this project is real, here is why we are doing it, here is roughly what it covers, and this person is in charge.' Someone senior, called the sponsor, signs it. The charter also lists assumptions, which are things we believe but have not proven, like 'the parts will arrive in a month', and constraints, which are firm limits, like 'we must finish by June'. It also says how we will know we succeeded, in numbers. Think of planning a wedding: the couple sets the budget and date (constraints) and hopes the weather is good (assumption).

Three weeks into the new warehouse scanner rollout at Cedar Freight, a functional manager refuses to release two of her technicians to your project. 'Who said this was a priority?' she asks. 'And who said you could book my people?' You start to explain that everyone knows the project was approved. She asks to see it in writing. You search your inbox and find a hallway conversation turned into a two-line email from the operations director, nothing more. No signed document, no stated budget, no named project manager. Suddenly you are not sure what authority you actually have. What should have existed before you booked a single person?

Initiating turns an approved idea into an official project. Its most important output is the project charter, a short document that formally authorizes the project, names the project manager and gives them authority to apply organizational resources to the work. The sponsor, or another person with authority outside the project, signs it. The project manager may help draft the charter, but they do not sign their own authorization, just as an employee does not approve their own expense report. Until the charter is signed, the project does not formally exist, and a project manager who starts spending money or booking people without one is taking a real risk.
