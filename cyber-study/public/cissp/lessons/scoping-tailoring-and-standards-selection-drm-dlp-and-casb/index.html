ISC2 CISSP 2024 outline · Domain 2: Asset security

Scoping, tailoring and standards selection; DRM, DLP and CASB

▶ Watch the overview video

Explain it simpler

Imagine a huge cookbook with a thousand recipes. You would never cook all of them. First you decide which cookbook fits your kitchen and your guests' needs (standards selection). Then you skip the recipes that need equipment you do not own, like a deep fryer (scoping). Finally, you adjust the recipes you keep, such as using less salt for a guest on a diet (tailoring). Security frameworks work the same way. Three tools then help protect information: DRM is like a lock that stays on a document even after you hand it to someone, DLP is like a guard at the door checking that nothing sensitive walks out, and a CASB is like a supervisor watching which online services employees use and what they put there.

Your first week as security lead at Meadowbrook Health Tech, the compliance manager drops a binder on your desk: hundreds of controls from a federal baseline that a government customer now requires. The engineers groan that half of them mention mainframes and wireless networks they do not have. That afternoon, a researcher asks how to stop partner companies from keeping copies of shared study documents after a contract ends, and a manager forwards an alert about patient files appearing in a file-sharing service nobody approved. Three problems, one theme: how do you pick and shape the right controls, and which tool closes which gap?

Frameworks and control catalogs, such as National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Special Publication (SP) 800-53, ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A or the CIS Critical Security Controls, list far more controls than any one system needs. Standards selection, scoping and tailoring are how you turn a generic catalog into the right set of controls for your environment. Done well, they save money, focus effort on real risks and give auditors a clear record of why each control is or is not in place.
