Data roles: owner, controller, processor, custodian, steward, subject

Explain it simpler

Picture a family that hires a moving company. The family decides what gets moved, where it goes and why; they are in charge, like a data owner or data controller. The movers carry the boxes exactly as instructed; like a data custodian or data processor, they do the hands-on work but do not choose what happens to the things. A family member who keeps the labels neat and makes sure every box is listed correctly is like a data steward. And the person whose belongings are in the boxes is like the data subject. If a box goes missing, the family still has to answer for it, even though the movers held it. Knowing who plays which part stops everyone from assuming someone else is taking care of the data.

A ticket lands in your queue at Riverbend Family Clinic: a patient wants a copy of every piece of personal data the clinic holds about her. Your manager says to forward it to the cloud scheduling company, since they store the data. The scheduling company's support agent replies that they only act on the clinic's instructions. The IT contractor says he runs the backups but has never decided who can see anything. Everyone touches the data, nobody seems to own it, and the legal clock on the request is already running. Who is actually responsible here?

Knowing who is responsible for what is central to asset security, and the exam frequently describes a person's duties and asks which role they hold. The roles come from two overlapping vocabularies. Organizational security uses owner, custodian, steward and user. Privacy law, especially the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), uses controller, processor and subject. One organization, or even one person, can hold roles from both vocabularies at once.
