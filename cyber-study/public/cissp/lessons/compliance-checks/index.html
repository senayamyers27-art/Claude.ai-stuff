Compliance checks

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 7, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Compliance means following a set of rules you must or have agreed to follow: laws, contracts with customers, industry standards or your own company policies. A compliance check confirms you are actually following them and collects proof. Some checks are automatic, like a tool that compares every computer's settings against an approved list. Others look at whether people followed the process, like checking training records. Being compliant is not the same as being safe. Passing a driving test does not mean you will never crash. The rules are a minimum, so a smart company meets them and then manages its other risks too. When a rule cannot be met, the company writes down why, adds another protection, and has a leader formally accept the risk.

The PCI assessor arrives at Copperline Outfitters in three weeks, and the store manager, Ray, is calm because the company passed last year. You are not so calm. Since then, two point-of-sale servers were rebuilt by a contractor, a new hire skipped security training, and a storage bucket was created in the cloud for a promotion. Nobody has checked any of them against the standard. Ray asks why you are worried when they passed before. How do you show, with evidence, that every requirement is still met, and what happens if one is not?

Compliance checks verify that systems, processes and people conform to the requirements an organization must meet or has chosen to meet. Those requirements come from laws and regulations, contracts such as the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) for organizations that handle card data, industry frameworks, and the organization's own policies, standards and baselines. The purpose is to produce evidence that required controls exist and operate as intended, and to find gaps before an auditor, a regulator or an attacker does.
