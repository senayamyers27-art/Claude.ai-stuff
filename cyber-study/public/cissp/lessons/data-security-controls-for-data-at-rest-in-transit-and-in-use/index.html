ISC2 CISSP 2024 outline · Domain 2: Asset security

Data security controls for data at rest, in transit and in use

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 7, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Information is always in one of three situations. It can be sitting still, like money in a safe (data at rest). It can be traveling, like money in an armored truck (data in transit). Or it can be in someone's hands being counted (data in use). Each situation needs a different kind of protection. The safe needs a strong lock and a carefully kept key. The truck needs armor and a trusted route. The counting room needs trustworthy staff, cameras and the rule that each person only touches what they need. Information is usually most exposed while it is being used, because it has to be unlocked to work with. Good security covers all three, because thieves go wherever the guard is weakest.

Marcus from the sales team at Bluewater Consulting calls the help desk from the back of a taxi. He left his laptop in the previous cab, and he is not worried, because the security team told him it is encrypted. Then he adds that he had just closed the lid to put it to sleep. You check the device record: full-disk encryption is on, but the laptop was unlocked and sleeping when it went missing. Across the building, an auditor is asking why card numbers appear in plain text in a customer service screen. Is the data protected or not, and what does protected even mean in each of these moments?

Data exists in three states, and each needs different protections. Data at rest is stored: on disks, in databases, on backup tapes or in cloud storage. Data in transit, also called data in motion, is moving across a network, whether inside a data center or across the internet. Data in use is being actively processed in memory by an application or viewed by a user. A strong program covers all three, because attackers go wherever protection is weakest, and the same record passes through every state many times a day.
