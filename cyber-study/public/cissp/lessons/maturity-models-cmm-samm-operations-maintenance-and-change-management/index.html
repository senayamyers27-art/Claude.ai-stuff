Maturity models: CMM, SAMM; operations, maintenance and change management

Explain it simpler

A maturity model is a ladder that describes how grown-up a team's way of working is. On the bottom rung, things get done only because a few talented people work hard, and results are unpredictable. Higher up, the team writes down how it works, everyone follows the same steps, results are measured, and the team keeps improving the process itself. Think of a restaurant: one where the chef cooks from memory and every plate tastes different, versus one with written recipes, timers, and a manager who tracks complaints and adjusts the recipes. Software security works the same way. After software is released, it still needs care: watching it, fixing it, and making every change through a careful, approved process so nothing breaks by surprise.

It is Monday morning at Lakeshore Payroll Systems, and the new chief information security officer has asked every development lead one question: how do you know your next release will be as secure as your last one? The mobile team says their best engineer checks everything personally. The web team shows a binder of procedures nobody has opened in a year. The platform team pulls up a dashboard of scan results and fix times. Then someone mentions that last Friday's hotfix went straight to production without a single test. Three teams, three very different answers. How do you describe where each one stands, and how do you decide what to improve first?

Maturity models measure how well developed and repeatable an organization's processes are, and they give a roadmap for improvement. For software security, the CISSP exam focuses on the Capability Maturity Model (CMM) lineage and on the Software Assurance Maturity Model (SAMM), and it links them to the discipline of operating and changing software safely after release. The core idea is that mature, defined and measured processes produce more predictable, higher-quality and more secure software than heroic individual effort. A team that depends on one expert is only as secure as that person's attention on a given day.
