Explain it simpler

Business continuity planning is preparing ahead of time so a business can keep its most important work going when something bad happens, like a fire, flood or cyberattack. First you figure out which activities matter most and how much it hurts to lose each one over time. That study is called a business impact analysis. From it come a few key numbers: the longest the business can survive without an activity, how fast you aim to get it back, and how much recent data you can afford to lose. Think of a family planning for a power outage. They decide the fridge can stay off about a day before food spoils, aim to get a generator running within a few hours, and keep a list of what matters most. People's safety always comes first.

The storm took out power to the Cedar Valley Distribution warehouse at 11 p.m., and by 2 a.m. the generator has failed too. You are on the phone with the operations vice president, who has one question: 'How long until orders are flowing again?' Someone mentions the backup server in the other building. Someone else remembers that backups only run on Sundays. Nobody can say how many hours the company can survive without shipping, or how many orders it can afford to lose. You realize these answers should have been decided months ago, calmly, in a conference room. What should that planning have produced?

Business continuity planning (BCP) keeps critical business functions running, or restores them quickly, during and after a disruption. Disaster recovery (DR) is the narrower, more technical part focused on restoring IT systems and data. The CISSP exam treats BCP as a business-led effort: senior management must sponsor it, business units must participate, and the first priority in any disaster is the safety of people. Systems and data always come after human life. The exam will often give you a disaster scenario with several plausible actions, and the one that protects people, such as evacuating or accounting for staff, is the best first answer whenever lives could be at risk.
