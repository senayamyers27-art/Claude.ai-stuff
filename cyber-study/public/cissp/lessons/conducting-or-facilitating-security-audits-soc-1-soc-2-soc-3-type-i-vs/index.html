ISC2 CISSP 2024 outline · Domain 6: Security assessment & testing

Conducting or facilitating security audits: SOC 1/SOC 2/SOC 3, Type I vs Type II

▶ Watch the overview video

Explain it simpler

When a company hands work to an outside provider, like payroll or cloud hosting, it needs proof that the provider is careful. Instead of every customer inspecting the provider, the provider hires an independent accounting firm to check and write a report everyone can rely on. A SOC 1 report is about money matters that affect the customer's financial statements. A SOC 2 report is the detailed one about security and related areas, usually shared only under a confidentiality agreement. A SOC 3 is a short public summary of the same areas. Type I means the auditor checked that controls were designed well on one day. Type II means the auditor checked that they actually worked over several months, which is stronger proof.

Procurement at Tidewater Logistics wants to sign a contract with a cloud HR platform by Friday. The vendor's sales rep has sent a glossy SOC 3 badge and a link to a public summary, and promises a "full audit" is available. Your payroll team separately needs something for the external financial auditors about the payroll provider. Leila in procurement asks the obvious question: "They have a SOC report. Isn't that enough?" You know there are three SOC reports and two types. Which ones do you actually need, and what will you look for once you have them?

When an organization relies on a service provider such as a payroll processor, cloud host or data center, it usually cannot audit that provider's controls itself. It would be impractical for thousands of customers to send auditors to the same provider, and the provider could not operate if they did. Instead, the provider engages an independent auditor to evaluate its controls and issue a report that customers can rely on. These are System and Organization Controls (SOC) reports, issued by certified public accountants under attestation standards from the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA). Internationally, a similar attestation exists under the ISAE 3402 standard. The exam focuses on the three SOC report types and on Type I versus Type II.
