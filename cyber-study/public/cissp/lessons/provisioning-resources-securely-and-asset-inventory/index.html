ISC2 CISSP 2024 outline · Domain 2: Asset security

Provisioning resources securely and asset inventory

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 7, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Think about moving into a new apartment with a box of keys from the landlord. If you do not know which key opens which door, or how many doors there even are, you cannot be sure the place is locked. An asset inventory is the list of every device, program and cloud service an organization has, who is responsible for each one, and where it is. Secure provisioning means that every new item is set up safely before anyone starts using it: bought from a trusted seller, default passwords changed, updates installed and added to the list. Together they make sure nothing important is forgotten, because a forgotten computer never gets updated and becomes an easy way in for attackers.

It is 7 a.m. at Pinecrest Logistics and the security news feed is full of a critical flaw in a remote access appliance. The chief information officer messages you one line: how many of these do we have, and who owns them? You open the asset spreadsheet. It was last updated eleven months ago, lists two appliances, and has an owner column full of blanks. Meanwhile someone in marketing mentions the cloud server they set up with a company card last spring. You realize the real question is not how fast you can patch, but whether you even know what needs patching.

Every new laptop, server, cloud account, container image or software license is a new asset to protect. Secure provisioning means bringing those resources into the environment in a known, approved and trackable state. Asset inventory means knowing at all times what you have, who owns it, where it is and what state it is in. Together they prevent the unknown, unmanaged systems that attackers love to find.
